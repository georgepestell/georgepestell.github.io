In Defence of Albums10/10/2026
Created: 10/10/2026Modified: 23/10/2026

When I was 16, Bon Iver was my most listened to Artist. Then again at 17, 18, 19, (not 20), 21, and 22 before I stopped having any data, Bon Iver has consistently been my most listened to artist for most of my adolescent and technically-an-adult life.

The way I generally listen to music is Albums. The story and flow a well crafted album can emanate brings another texture to the best songs, and raises the worst to a point where my favourite albums have little to no unintentional low points. Many of the songs of My Dark Beautiful Fantasy (Ye, 2012) are ones that I would generally skip if they came on a playlist or shuffled into what I am listening to. But hearing the Aphex Twin vibes of TODO sandwiched between TODO and TODO, understanding the context of TODO, you're draw in, and the song becomes a little more.

Bon Iver in my opinion is one of the best Album crafters in the industry. When the singles for the latest 2024 album were released, I considered them to be soft, easy-listening, and I mistakenly wrote the album off when it released not giving it a real chance.

After some time, after another run of their discography, I reluctantly gave it another go. To my surprise, something had changed, and suddenly I found myself understand the message of TODO isn't a cry for folks to get on. It's not a ballad about TODO, but a TODO.

The listening experience of bands like Pink Floyd and Led Zeppelin put them on the map. With each new release becoming the main event of the year. It's hard to under-estimate the influence an aritst's format has on the story-telling and complexity of the result: when creating a non-LP single, sure you can paint a beutiful picture of a moment in time, but with an LP, the movie comes alive, with conversation, movement, and subversion.

I have no love for the streaming platforms that almost all of us use as the primary interface with the music that we love and hope to one day find. It's becoming clearer that the argument of platform is less of an invitation, and more of a demand these days, with most artists getting new listeners through viral moments on social media than discovery algorithms on streaming services. What once seemed an incredible way to expand your horizons, it has become obvious that instead they promote the same small number of increasingly literally not-real artists as everybody with your preference category.

This is a problem with the algorithm design itself. The phenomenon of siloisation is somewhat inevitable given the fact that they merely try to maximise the likelihood you'll listen to a song given the sets of songs you have listened to previously, and how people with similar listening histories went on to listen to. There cannot be any true growth or development in a world in which these algortihms are the primary method of finding new music. You will always be late to the party, you will always be unsurprised.
