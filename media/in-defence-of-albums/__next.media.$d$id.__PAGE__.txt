<p>I have no love for the streaming platforms that almost all of us use as the primary interface with the music that we love and hope to one day find. It's becoming clearer that the argument of platform is less of an invitation, and more of a demand these days, with most artists getting new listeners through viral moments on social media than discovery algorithms on streaming services. What once seemed an incredible way to expand your horizons, it has become obvious that instead they promote the same small number of increasingly literally not-real artists as everybody with your preference category.</p>
<p>This is a problem with the algorithm design itself. The phenomenon of siloisation is somewhat inevitable given the fact that they merely try to maximise the likelihood you'll listen to a song given the sets of songs you have listened to previously, and how people with similar listening histories went on to listen to. There cannot be any true growth or development in a world in which these algortihms are the primary method of finding new music. You will always be late to the party, you will always be unsurprised.</p>
